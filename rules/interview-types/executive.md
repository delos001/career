# Interview type: Executive / Leadership

## Identity
- Late-stage, typically post-HM and post-panel: meeting a leadership-team member (skip-level, VP, or executive). Often the final gate before an offer.
- Fit is largely settled. The round is about strategic alignment, culture-add, and whether the candidate operates credibly with leadership.
- The most bidirectional round: the executive often sells the company as much as vets the candidate. Prime moment to resolve the candidate's remaining big-picture concerns and to assess the leadership itself.

## Emphasis
- Up a level from role mechanics: strategy, business context, leadership philosophy, why-this-company-at-this-inflection.
- Leans on the shared main body (Positioning, Why-this-company; most Concerns already resolved). The Appendix stays thin.
- Show executive presence: facts fast, top-down, a point of view.

## Research targets (for the research pass)
- Interviewer intel on the executive: remit, background, public statements, priorities, initiatives they own. Executives usually have a richer public footprint (talks, interviews, posts), so deeper research is worthwhile.
- Company strategic direction and recent leadership / org moves.

## Question Bank priorities
- Strategic questions to ask THEM: where the org and function are heading; what success looks like at the org level; how this role fits the strategy; leadership's view of the modernization / agenda; what keeps them up at night.
- Fewer role-mechanics questions (resolved in earlier rounds).

## Concerns to push here
- The remaining big-picture concerns: is the org genuinely committed to the direction (modernization fit); is the mandate real and resourced; leadership quality and culture at the top; growth trajectory.
- Assess, do not just answer: does the candidate want to work for and with this leadership.

## Posture
- Peer-to-leadership: strategic, concise, decisive. Engage as someone who could sit at that table. Have a point of view.

## Avoid
- Dropping into role mechanics or technical minutiae (wrong altitude).
- Rehashing why-leave defensively, or over-selling (fit is settled; this is mutual alignment now).
- Being passive: executives expect the candidate to have views and questions. Do not recite company facts back at them.
