# Interview type: Recruiter

## Identity
- A recruiter or talent partner screens for basic fit and coordinates the process; usually not the decision-maker on role substance.
- Usually the first round, but it can follow a hiring-manager round when the manager sourced the candidate directly and then hands off to recruiting.

## Emphasis
- Lead with **Compensation / Logistics**: numbers confirmed this run, a scope-conditional stated range, market data cited from the research ledger; logistics conditional facts confirmed this run, static facts from user-info.md shown to confirm.
- Keep **Fit and Gaps** at headline level; a recruiter rarely goes deep on proof-point stories or situational questions.
- If earlier rounds have happened, keep this round consistent with what was said in them: point to the existing main body and add only what this round needs.

## Research targets (for the research pass)
- Compensation calibration (skip if a posted band and the user's numbers settle it).
- Interview-process intel.
- JD internal-reference decode (company shorthand; one per phrase found).
- Company mission and core values: normally captured at role-intake in research.md's Company section; top up only if intake predated that capture or left them blank. Some employers screen explicitly against their values, so this grounds natural values-fit conversation.

## Question Bank priorities
- Process, timeline, and next steps.
- Flag which Question Bank items to hold for later rounds with people who have real authority over the role.

## Concerns to push here
- Only those a recruiter can actually resolve: process, timing, compensation range, logistics.
- Mark anything a recruiter says about the role itself as second-hand, to confirm with the hiring manager.

## Posture
- Concise and easy to advance. Give the recruiter clean answers they can pass on.

## Avoid
- Pressing a recruiter on questions only the hiring manager can answer.
- Treating recruiter statements about role scope as fact.
