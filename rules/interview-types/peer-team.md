# Interview type: Peer / Team

## Identity
- Interviews with future peers or team members the candidate would work alongside (not the manager, not an executive).
- Assesses collaboration fit, day-to-day working style, and hands-on / technical credibility: "would I want to work next to this person, and can they pull their weight?"
- Peers hold real influence (often a veto) and the most honest view of the team and the role. Frequently several 1:1s or a shared panel.

## Emphasis
- Collaboration and working style; hands-on technical credibility (peers test whether the candidate can actually do the work); the real day-to-day from the people who live it; team culture.
- Less "why this company," more "how do you work and how do you handle X."
- A strong cross-check round: re-ask the role-scope and day-to-day questions here to compare answers against the HR and HM versions.

## Research targets (for the research pass)
- Interviewer intel per peer (role, tenure, what they own). Peers usually have thin public footprints, so lean on confirm-live handling + an opener rather than padded guesses.
- Team composition and how the roles interlock.

## Question Bank priorities
- How the team actually works day to day; collaboration patterns across the adjacent functions; what is genuinely hard about the role or team; the real tooling / stack; what they wish they had more help with.
- Role-scope and hands-on / oversight questions (re-asked here as a cross-check on earlier answers).
- The opener that invites the peer's own path and view of the team.

## Concerns to push here
- Day-to-day reality and whether the role is genuinely hands-on (peers give the unvarnished version).
- Team culture and health; cross-checking the HM's claims.
- Not comp (never with peers); not high-level strategy (that is the executive round).

## Posture
- Collegial, curious, generous with credit. Demonstrate being a good teammate: technical peer-to-peer without being competitive; competence with humility.

## Avoid
- Talking down to peers, over-asserting seniority, or treating them as less important than the HM (they influence the decision).
- Comp or leveling talk; badmouthing past teams; interrogating rather than conversing.
