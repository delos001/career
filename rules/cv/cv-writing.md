# CV Writing - How Content Is Chosen and Written

**Used by:** cv-targeted.

**Purpose.** The CV must persuade a reviewer to invite the candidate for an interview. Every rule in this file serves that purpose.

**Claims.** Every claim cites the profile entries that support it. A claim may reword or combine entries. A combined claim must cite each individual entry it draws on. No claim may state more than its entries show (`global-rules.md`). A fact the candidate gave in this application's gap analysis that is not yet in the inventory is cited by its requirement (`<!-- src: GAP CR-005 -->`), only when that requirement's status in this application's `gap_analysis.md` is `closed` or `partial-match`, and the claim may not exceed its Notes there.

**How to read this file.** This file says how CV content is chosen and written; `cv-structure.md` says which sections exist, their order, their line layouts and the length limits. A section headed `## KEY: name` applies to one drafting step; a section without a key applies to every step. The five axis files are used to align the CV's prose with the job's orientation, industry, specialty, level and work state. A rule marked `(hard rule)` always applies; no reviewer suggestion overrides it.

## PLAN: Selection

The plan step decides what goes on the CV before any section is written. It works from `jd_needs.md`, which lists the job's requirements and groups them into ranked needs (N-01 is the highest), and from the profile entries retrieval found for each need.

**Read profile entries for what the candidate did.** A profile entry is a general description of work, not text to copy or to match word for word. For each entry, identify the core work done, function performed, goal reached or impact made, setting aside its contextual details. Then judge which needs that experience answers, considering whether its function, goal or impact is equivalent or transferable to what the job needs. Example: a JD asks for oversight of CROs; the candidate oversaw laboratory and imaging vendors. That oversight is a good substitute for what the JD asks, so the entry is chosen, and the bullet names the vendors the candidate actually oversaw, never CROs. Never choose an entry because its words match the JD, and never pass one over because they do not.

**Choose content one ranked need at a time.** Work through the ranked needs in `jd_needs.md`, highest first. For each need, decide which entries best show that the candidate has done that work, reached that goal or had that impact, and which role each claim sits under. The writer makes this judgment; retrieval scores only find entries and break ties between entries that serve a need equally well.

**Relevance decides, never the size of a figure.** Choose a claim for the need it serves. Never choose one claim over another because its figure (a percentage, count or amount) is larger.

**Cover every required requirement.** Every requirement marked required in `jd_needs.md` must be present in the CV, in whichever section fits it. One bullet or item may cover several of them.

**Strengths the JD does not ask for.** A strength or experience the JD does not specify gets a bullet only when an outcome in `jd_needs.md`, the level file and tier, or another axis file makes it persuasive for this job. When it does, the plan states the specific reason.

**Record the choices in `drafting_plan.md`.** For each need, list the claims chosen and cite the profile entries each claim draws on. For each role older than the Professional Experience window, record whether it is included because it closes a gap no newer role closes. For each role inside the window, record whether it gets bullets or a one-to-two-line summary. When a reviser could not work out a choice from its need and citations alone, record a one-line reason for it: for example, when a strength beyond the JD is included, an older role is included, or a lower-ranked need is kept over a higher one.

**Revisers keep the plan.** A later writer or reviser keeps each recorded choice unless a reviewer or QC finding challenges it.

**Draft long, then cut.** The first draft is deliberately longer than the CV can be. Its goal is a complete set of persuasive, relevant options for every need, so the later cut has good options to choose from. Tag every bullet with the need it serves (`<!-- need: N-NN -->`) beside its source citation. A later step cuts the draft to length under the length rules in `cv-structure.md`, starting with the lowest-ranked need.

## SUM: Professional Summary

The summary is the first thing a reviewer reads. In a few seconds it must persuade the reviewer that the candidate has done the core work this job exists to do, so the reviewer keeps reading.

**Content.** Build each sentence from one key item of the role statement in `jd_needs.md`, or two closely related items. State that the candidate has done that work, summarized across roles, and anchor it in a detail particular to the candidate's experience (a domain, setting, result or impact), so the sentence does not merely restate the JD. Detailed results, and descriptions of methods, tools or individual projects, belong in the bullets; the summary states the work at a high level. Every kind of work the summary names must appear in at least one bullet. Mention current work only where it maps to a job need.

**Figures in the summary (hard rule).** Besides the career span, the summary may carry at most 2 figures, and only when each directly shows a job requirement or a scale signal the level file names. Pair each figure with words that give its scope ("30-person global study team"). A figure a bullet already carries is never repeated in the summary; the summary states that impact in words. Never add a figure to reach the limit.

**Career span.** State a span of years only when a job requirement asks for years of experience. Match that requirement's field and threshold: a requirement for "15+ years in clinical data management" gets "over 15 years in clinical data management," never the candidate's total career years. A long total span signals age, which lowers callbacks; show breadth in words instead ("global and multi-regional study teams"). The writer names the positions in that field, and a script computes their span from the dates in `inventory.md`; if the span falls short of the threshold, state no span. Never copy a span from a previous CV.

## COMP: Core Competencies

Core Competencies persuades a reviewer, at a glance, that the candidate has the capabilities this job asks for. Each item is a short phrase naming one capability, in the JD's terms where they accurately describe the candidate's capability. A capability shown only in the candidate's Independent Projects may appear when the job asks for it (e.g. "AI-driven USDM and regulation knowledge graphing").

**What qualifies.** Include a capability only when it answers something the JD or `jd_needs.md` asks for. It may be an equivalent or transferable capability (a JD asking for leading a team of ten is answered by "functional and technical team leadership"). Never pad toward the ceiling.

## EXP: Professional Experience

Professional Experience persuades the reviewer that the candidate has done the work this job needs. The roles that serve the job's highest-ranked needs get the most depth.

**Every role in Professional Experience is evidence.** When any role in Professional Experience, including the earliest, shows a job need, especially ownership, accountability or adoption, its bullet says so with the scope that proves it, even when the role carries one bullet.

**Bullet order.** Within a role, keep related bullets together so they read as one area of breadth. Order the groups and single bullets by the needs they serve, highest-ranked first; a group takes the place of its highest-ranked bullet.

### Bullet rules

These rules apply to every bullet on the CV, including role summaries and project bullets.

**Each bullet shows its match to a need.** A bullet names the function it shows the way the JD names that kind of work. Open with the function when the match is the selling point ("Led development of a reusable dashboard template..."), or with the result when the result draws the reviewer more and still shows the function. Beyond what the candidate did, include only the one or two of challenge, context and result that make the bullet most persuasive and clear; no part is required at any level. Cut detail that serves no need.

**State what was done.** Every bullet states what the candidate did or achieved. "Responsible for X" and lists of duties are not bullets.

**Figures.** Keep a figure when it shows the need being met. When a bullet runs long, cut other words first and the persuasive figure last. Never invent a number.

**One sentence per bullet (hard rule).** Each bullet is one sentence. Two accomplishments become two bullets; one accomplishment written as two sentences becomes one.

**Bullet length (hard rule).** A bullet is at most 2 lines. A 3-line bullet is a rare exception, and the plan records why; a bullet never runs to 4 lines. QC flags every bullet estimated at 3 lines as a finding to shorten. A bullet carries one main claim and at most one or two supporting details; cut true detail the claim does not need. A starting point ("from roughly 60% to zero") stays when it is the impact and the bullet still fits in 2 lines; otherwise give the end result. Test: a smart reader outside the candidate's field gets the point in one read.

**Combining entries into one bullet.** Where related entries together show broader scope or responsibility, combine them into one claim. Whether to combine follows the level file: leadership reads as breadth and reach (`rules/levels/leadership.md`), so combining usually helps; individual contributor work reads as depth (`rules/levels/ic.md`), and a need asking for varied work keeps varied bullets. A combined bullet draws only on entries from the role it sits under. A theme that spans employers belongs in the summary. A narrative (ST-NNN) may shape how a claim is framed; it never selects entries, and it never lets a bullet draw on another role's entries.

## PROJ: Independent Projects

Independent Projects persuades the reviewer that the candidate's skills are current and hands-on.

**Voice.** Describe a project as work product: what was designed, built, analyzed or delivered. The leadership voice used in Professional Experience does not apply here, whatever the CV's level.

**Writing project bullets.** Project bullets follow every bullet rule. A project appears as its objective, framed for the job, with its relevant elements folded in. Each objective gets one bullet by default; it gets a second only when its elements serve different job needs, and each bullet cites the objective. Never write one bullet per element. A bullet that uses an element cites it with its objective (`PR-015, PR-015.03`), and the element's Outcome is the source for its impact.

## Writing rules

These rules apply to every section of the CV.

**Reads clearly on the first pass (hard rule).** Every summary sentence and bullet is a complete, natural sentence that a hiring manager who has never seen the source understands on one read, and it shows why the work mattered. A sentence that makes sense only to someone who already knows the work fails, however accurate it is. When a clear sentence will not fit, drop a fact, a list item or a whole bullet; never drop the words that make it read clearly. Clarity outranks adding keywords or one more fact. When clarity forces a required requirement out of a sentence, cover it in another bullet or section.

- Fails: "Delivered dashboards that study teams used to decide in meetings and centralized data surveillance with real-time findings, and drove self-service analytics adoption."
- Passes: "Replaced weekly spreadsheet reporting with near-real-time dashboards, so study teams could make decisions from current data."

**Natural phrasing.** Write the way a professional would say it. Use standard word pairings ("advise against," not "advise out of"). State a benefit plainly ("make decisions from current data"), never as a staged scene ("decided in the meeting," "a team member took it over"). Use plain verbs for what the candidate did ("interviewed and provided recommendations on 50+ candidates, resulting in ... hires," not "evaluated 50+ candidates behind ... hires"). An odd phrase stops the reader, and a staged one invites doubt.

**The JD's term when it is accurate.** When the JD has its own term for work the candidate did, use the JD's term if and only if it accurately describes that work; otherwise keep the profile's term. Example: an entry says "data conformance," and the JD calls the same work "programming and reporting," so the bullet says "programming and reporting." Never use a JD term that stretches the claim or adds a keyword the work does not support.

**Plain, verifiable language (hard rule).** No keyword stuffing, no verbs inflated beyond the level file's voice, and no duty-list phrasing.

**No contrasts that read as critical.** Leave out contrasts that could read as critical of other approaches, past employers or usual practice ("through matrix influence rather than headcount"); those points belong in the interview, where tone and context can be given. A plain outcome stays ("absorbed 200+ transfers without headcount growth" reports a result and judges no one).

**Transferable experience.** An entry whose industry or specialty is not adjacent to the job's (per that axis file's Adjacency section) is used only for a capability that crosses fields, such as leading people, building a capability or improving a process, and is never presented as industry or specialty experience the candidate lacks. An adjacent entry follows "The JD's term when it is accurate."

**Acronym expansion.** Spell out a less-common acronym on first use, with the acronym in parentheses; use the acronym thereafter. Common in-industry acronyms the hiring panel reads fluently (e.g. FDA, GCP, ICH, IRB, GxP in pharma) need no expansion. Judge "common" by the job's industry, not the general reader.

**No em dashes (hard rule).** Em dashes do not appear in the CV. Use a pipe (|) or hyphen for header separators (Company | Location | Dates), and rewrite prose and bullets with periods, semicolons, or natural connectives instead of em-dash clause breaks.

**No AI-tell phrasing (hard rule).** The CV must not read as machine-written. (Source: Wikipedia, "Signs of AI writing.") Avoid:
- Negation-contrast framing: "not just X, but Y," "it's not X, it's Y," "less about X and more about Y." State the point directly.
- Rule-of-three padding: do not force a third item (adjective, adjective, adjective; phrase, phrase, and phrase) for rhythm. List only what is real.
- Trailing significance clauses: the appended "-ing" flourish ("..., driving efficiency," "..., streamlining operations") that asserts vague impact. Replace it with the concrete result, or cut it.
- Buzzword/puffery used as filler: leverage, robust, seamless, pivotal, crucial, foster, underscore, showcase, groundbreaking, world-class, cutting-edge, transformative (and "spearhead" unless the level file's verb vocabulary lists it). "Pivotal" is allowed in its trial sense ("pivotal study," "pivotal trial," "pivotal Phase 3," "pivotal program"). Prefer the level axis file's verb vocabulary and concrete language.
- Marketing copulas: "serves as," "stands as," "is designed to" in place of plain "is/was" or an action verb.
- Vague intensifiers standing in for a result: significantly, greatly, successfully, substantially. State the concrete result, or state the work plainly.
- Title Case headings and decorative boldface: labels use the defined heading style, not Title Case On Every Word.

**Each achievement has one home (hard rule).** Each distinct achievement, a specific result or named accomplishment ("200+ data transfers without headcount growth," "selected Databricks as the enterprise platform"), appears as one bullet, under the role that owns it, and is never restated as a second bullet under the same or another role. The summary may name an achievement's impact in words. A figure appears in only one place. A bullet keeps the figures that show its need; the summary carries a figure only when no bullet carries it. A Core Competencies item never restates a figure or accomplishment carried by the summary or a bullet.

**Protect the most recent role.** The most recent role in Professional Experience gets the most attention, so each of its bullets carries a distinct achievement. When one initiative (for example, building a new function) spans several related entries, do not spend one bullet per facet (operating model, team, throughput, roadmap) repeating a shared qualifier ("greenfield," "without proportional headcount"); combine the facets into one claim where the level file supports combining (see Combining entries into one bullet); otherwise keep separate bullets and state the shared qualifier once.

**Cutting words can create an overstatement.** Qualifier words carry claims. Removing "a version of," "contributed to" or "supported" can turn an accurate claim into an overstatement without changing a fact. After any cut, run the claim-support check (`qc-cv-targeted`) again, as well as `cv_qc.py`. When a cut would remove a qualifier, keep the qualifier and cut other words in the same bullet.
