# Leadership - CV Framing Rules

**Used by:** cv-targeted, axis-classifier

## Identity

The CV is framed as organizational leadership and the building or governance of capabilities, teams, and functions. Headline content reads as: cross-functional or portfolio ownership, organizational decision-making, capability-building, governance frameworks, multi-function influence, headcount and budget responsibility.

Select this level when the target role carries people-management authority, organizational scope, or multi-function leadership. Do not select when the role's core work is individual technical execution and it carries none of these (use ic). When a role has both, leadership takes precedence. Organizational scope includes owning a platform, capability, or standards for a whole function (for example, a function-wide analytics platform or coding standards), even when the role also builds it hands-on. It also includes work that spans a major organizational unit or several functions (for example, advancing initiatives across all of R&D or partnering with Research, Development, Finance and BD), even when final decisions sit above the role. Judge by where the work lands, not by verb strength: "support", "advance", "contribute" and "partner" imply no level on their own. Job title and a JD's silence on direct reports do not decide the level.

## Voice

Strategic and outcome-focused. The work being described is organizational: decisions made, capabilities built, teams scaled, functions led, cross-functional influence exerted. The unit of work is organizational, not individual. The register rises with tier: apply the block for the tier recorded with the classification (`- Tier:` in the session log). A tier's verbs and avoid-list take precedence over the general Verb vocabulary below.

### Tiers

Assign the tier from the job's scope (how far the work reaches, who makes the final call), with the title as a weak hint only: Associate Director is the least reliable title (posted with Senior Manager at some companies, with Director at others), "Head of" names a function and carries no tier by itself, and small-company titles run higher than large-pharma ones. No public pharma-specific leveling standard defines these boundaries (Radford and Mercer definitions are paywalled); this four-tier cut is a synthesis from Carta's framework, practitioner sources, and Radford's executive band starting at VP, researched 2026-11.

- **delivery-lead** (typically Manager to Senior Manager): runs a study, trial or workstream to someone else's plan. Foreground delivery against plan: timelines, quality, the metrics owned. Verbs: managed, delivered, coordinated, implemented. Avoid: "set strategy", "drove the portfolio" (reads inflated).
- **function-lead** (typically Associate Director to Director): owns the outcome for a program, multi-study area or function with little supervision, and makes the trade-offs inside it; includes work advancing initiatives across a major unit. Foreground outcomes owned, the decisions behind them, their consequences. Verbs: led, owned, built, established, directed. Avoid: task-level detail (reads junior); enterprise or board language (reads senior).
- **multi-function-head** (typically Senior Director to Executive Director): leads several functional areas through managers, owns the function's budget and plan, shapes the plan leadership approves. Foreground the operating model, building the organization, the function's strategy. Verbs: built, scaled, restructured, defined, aligned. Avoid: single-study detail. This boundary has the weakest evidence of the four.
- **executive** (VP, SVP, C-suite, head of R&D): sets the plan and answers for it, through several layers of management. Foreground the plan set and enterprise results: pipeline value, filings, financing, transformation, P&L. Verbs: set, shaped, positioned, secured, transformed. Avoid: project-execution bullets (reads Director). At C-suite the difference is mainly audience (board, investors), not verbs.

## Verb vocabulary

Reads as level-appropriate: championed, orchestrated, spearheaded, aligned, redesigned, established, governed, restructured, scaled, transformed, instituted, institutionalized.

Reads as under-leveled: developed, built, implemented (without organizational framing), supported, executed (as primary verb), troubleshot, refactored.

**Leadership is stated as "led."** Where the cited entries show the candidate led the work or the people, write "led," with no qualifier such as "operationally," "functionally" or "matrix-"; where it matters, the team or setting carries the detail ("led a global matrix team of seven data managers"). A qualified verb reads as "led, but not really." Any wording limit recorded in the candidate's role record still applies.

## Scope signals

Reads as leadership-level scope: cross-functional or portfolio ownership, organizational decision-making, capability-building, governance frameworks, multi-function influence, headcount and budget responsibility, multi-year horizon, regulatory or compliance scope at the organizational level.

Scale signals that establish seniority: portfolio breadth, vendor or partner scope, direct or matrix team size, budget envelope, regulatory or audit scope, organizational reach (functions affected, global vs. regional).

Reads as under-leveled: tool selection without organizational framing, individual deliverable execution, project-level scope without portfolio context, hands-on technical work as the primary subject of an achievement.

## Adjacency

Translation rule for entries tagged with ic when this level is selected for the CV:

- **ic**: translates when the entry's individual technical work can be re-anchored on a strategic outcome it enabled or a capability it built. Hands-on execution surfaced without organizational framing does not translate; it reads as scope inflation in reverse (leadership reframed as IC).
