# Leadership — CV Framing Rules

**Used by:** cv_targeted, role_evaluation

## Voice

Strategic and outcome-focused. The work being described is organizational: decisions made, capabilities built, functions led, cross-functional influence exerted. The unit of work is organizational, not individual. Director voice differs from Associate Director voice in scope and authority; both differ from VP and C-suite voice in horizon and breadth.

## Verb vocabulary

Reads as level-appropriate: championed, orchestrated, spearheaded, aligned, redesigned, established, governed, restructured, scaled, transformed, instituted, institutionalized.

Reads as under-leveled: developed, built, implemented (without organizational framing), supported, executed (as primary verb), troubleshot, refactored.

## Scope signals

Reads as leadership-level scope: cross-functional or portfolio ownership, organizational decision-making, capability-building, governance frameworks, multi-function influence, headcount and budget responsibility, multi-year horizon, regulatory or compliance scope at the organizational level.

Scale signals that establish seniority: portfolio breadth (studies, programs, therapeutic areas, product lines), vendor or partner scope, direct or matrix team size, budget envelope, regulatory or audit scope, organizational reach (functions affected, global vs. regional).

Reads as under-leveled: tool selection without organizational framing, individual deliverable execution, project-level scope without portfolio context, hands-on technical work as the primary subject of an achievement.

## Adjacency

A leader's hands-on technical work or individual deliverables translate as IC-level signal when surfaced without organizational framing. Such work is rarely useful in a leadership-positioned CV unless it explains how a strategic outcome was achievable. Surfacing it as primary content reads as scope inflation in reverse — leadership reframed as IC.
