# CRO - CV Framing Rules

**Used by:** cv_targeted, axis-classifier

## Vocabulary

Service-model terms: full-service outsourcing (FSO), functional service provider/provision (FSP), hybrid FSP/FSO (first-class delivery model 2024-2026; sponsors mix both across portfolios, with a 2024 survey reporting 41% of biopharma increased FSP use vs 27% for FSO). Newer named variants: Flexible FSP (Novotech), FSP 2.0 / FSP+ (FSP layered with consulting and analytics), blended FSP, flex-resourcing. Strategic partnership, preferred provider, co-development partner - labels for the deepest sponsor relationships.

Contract and commercial terms: master services agreement (MSA), statement of work (SOW) / work order, change order, time and materials, fixed-price / firm-fixed-price, unit-based pricing, milestone-based pricing, full-time equivalent (FTE), pass-through cost, scope creep, scope management, budget reconciliation, project profitability, clinical trial agreement (CTA - sponsor-CRO instrument; under FDA Sep 2024 DCT guidance must explicitly identify decentralized-element ownership), transfer of regulatory obligations (TORO - 21 CFR 312.52 written-agreement mechanism; ICH E6(R3) Section 3.9 codifies as service-provider transfer). Commercial-side: backlog, book-to-bill, burn rate, revenue recognition, net new awards, cancellations, win rate, bid-defense conversion.

Service-line terms: clinical operations, clinical monitoring (SQV/SIV/IMV/COV - site qualification, initiation, interim monitoring, close-out visits), risk-based monitoring (RBM), risk-based quality management (RBQM - R3-aligned umbrella replacing pure RBM), centralized monitoring, remote monitoring, source data verification (SDV), source data review (SDR), data management (DM), biostatistics, statistical programming (SAS, increasingly R), medical writing, regulatory affairs / regulatory operations, pharmacovigilance / drug safety (AE and SAE intake, MedDRA coding, CAPA workflows), QA / GCP audit, RTSM (Randomization and Trial Supply Management - current term; IRT / IWRS dated but recognized), central lab, bioanalytical (often under GCLP), eClinical (EDC, CTMS, eTMF, eCRF, eCOA, ePRO), real-world evidence (RWE) services, patient recruitment / site identification & feasibility, medical affairs / medical monitoring. Emerging 2025-2026 roles: AI Systems Coordinator, Clinical Informatics Manager, Automation Validation Expert.

Stakeholders: sponsor, CRO, FSP, FSO, vendor, site, IRB, IEC, DSMB, DMC, KOL, HCP. CRO-emphasis additions: sponsor (the CRO's customer - primary external stakeholder), business development (BD), capture management, proposal / bid defense, account management (AM), strategic account director / strategic partnership lead, project director (PD), project manager (PM), clinical trial manager (CTM), study lead, lead CRA, client services, alliance management, functional service head, functional lead, therapeutic area head.

Trial-lifecycle terms: study, trial, protocol, site, investigator (PI, sub-I), participant, patient, Phase I/II/III/IV, FIH, pivotal, registrational, arm, cohort, randomization, blinding (single-blind, double-blind, open-label), placebo-controlled, endpoint, screening, enrollment, indication, therapeutic area (TA). CRO-emphasis: trial conduct against contracted deliverables, sponsor-defined endpoints, sponsor SOP compliance (in addition to CRO SOPs), sponsor-mandated systems, joint review meetings, cycle-time metrics (FPI, LPI, LPLV, DBL-to-CSR), enrollment-vs-plan, site activation rate, monitoring visit compliance.

Submission terms: regulatory submission, pre-IND, IND, NDA, BLA, sBLA, ANDA, sNDA, MAA, 505(b)(2), CTD, eCTD, DMF. CDISC data standards (SDTM, ADaM, CDASH, Define-XML) on the data-package side.

Regulatory frameworks: GxP family (GCP, GMP, GLP, GDP, GVP), ICH (E6(R3) is current GCP standard, finalized Jan 2025 and now in active operational rollout with implementation maturity varying widely across CROs; E2A / E2B(R3) / E2D for clinical safety reporting; E3 CSR structure; E8(R1), E9(R1)), 21 CFR Part 11, EU Annex 11. Cross-jurisdictional regulator interaction is core CRO work: FDA, EMA, MHRA, PMDA, and NMPA are the agencies CROs file submissions to and host inspections from. EU CTR (Regulation 536/2014) is now fully applicable - transition period ended 31 January 2025, with all EU trials operating under CTR via CTIS. FDA BIMO compliance program 7348.810 governs sponsor and CRO inspections; FDA has increasingly issued Form 483s directly to sponsors and CROs for oversight failures rather than only to sites. CRO-emphasis: ICH E6(R3) Section 3.9 (sponsor oversight of service providers; sponsor accountability is explicit and non-delegable) replaces R2 Section 5.2 vocabulary. Key R3 phrasing: "service provider," "sponsor oversight," "fit-for-purpose," "risk-proportionate." For U.S. central-lab work, CLIA (42 CFR 493) and CAP accreditation.

Operating concepts: cross-sponsor program oversight; contract delivery against milestones and SLAs; resource utilization and billable hours; sponsor escalation; multi-protocol portfolio management; multi-TA capability; audit-readiness across multiple sponsor quality systems; risk-based quality management (RBQM) per E6(R3); critical-to-quality (CtQ) factors; quality tolerance limits (QTL); key risk indicators (KRI); centralized statistical monitoring (CSM); oversight plan; quality management plan (QMP); joint operating committee (JOC); joint steering committee (JSC); service-level agreement (SLA); key performance indicator (KPI); RACI; transition (study migration between CROs or in-housing); inspection-readiness (BIMO inspection passing, 483 observation and response cycle).

Non-preferred terms: "client" instead of "sponsor" in operational CV descriptions where "sponsor" is standard; "subject" (use "participant" - TransCelerate/NIH/NEJM standard applies on CRO side too); "IWRS" alone (pair with or replace by "RTSM"); ICH E6(R2) Section 5.2 phrasing as live framework (R3 supersedes); RBM alone where RBQM is the current umbrella; "fully decentralized trial" (FDA frames trials as having "decentralized elements" along a spectrum, not as a binary state); generic agency/consultancy verbs ("delivered for the client," "drove engagement") without trial-operational specifics; tech-industry verbs ("ship," "deploy," "iterate") in operational CV descriptions; "owned the protocol" (protocols are sponsor-owned; CRO staff operationalized, executed against, or managed conduct under the protocol).

Recognized acronyms in CRO hiring contexts: FDA, EMA, MHRA, PMDA, NMPA, NIH, BIMO, GxP, GCP, GCLP, GMP, GLP, GDP, GVP, ICH, IRB, IEC, IND, NDA, BLA, sBLA, ANDA, sNDA, MAA, CTA, CTD, eCTD, DMF, CRO, FSP, FSO, EDC, CTMS, eTMF, eCRF, IRT, IWRS, AE, SAE, MedDRA, CDISC, SDTM, ADaM, CDASH, CAPA, SOP, RBM, RBQM, DCT, RWE, KOL, HCP, DSMB, DMC, PI, FIH, MSA, SOW, CtQ, QTL, KRI, CSM, SDV, SDR, SQV, SIV, IMV, COV, FPI, LPI, LPLV, DBL, CSR, CRA, CTM, PM, PD, BD, AM, RTSM, eCOA, ePRO, JSC, JOC, SLA, KPI, RACI, QMP, QA, DM, SAS, AI, CDx, IVD, NEJM, SaaS, TORO, CTIS, CTR, FTE, ISO, CLIA, CAP, IVDR, MDR, IDE, PMA, PMP, CGT, CNS, TA, CFR, NPV, PPV, LoD, LoQ, RSABE, HVD, PK, BE, QMS, NSCLC, ICON.

## Emphasis

Hiring-panel emphasis falls on: cross-sponsor portfolio breadth (number of sponsors served, range of TAs and trial phases, ability to context-switch across sponsor SOPs - breadth across multiple sponsors in the same TA reads stronger than depth in a single pharma program); contract delivery and SLA achievement (on-time milestone delivery, scope-vs-actuals, change-order management, utilization rate, billable-hours ratio for FSP, study budget under management, backlog and revenue contribution); regulatory milestone delivery with concrete scope (FPI dates, DBL on plan, CSR delivered for NDA submission); inspection outcomes (BIMO passing, EMA inspection passed with no critical findings, 483 observation and the response and closure cycle); ICH E6(R3) currency including operational rollout specifics (RBQM operationalization, CtQ identification, QTL set and monitored, KRI defined and acted on) - phrasing entries against R3 Section 3.9 rather than legacy R2 Section 5.2 signals current literacy; sponsor-CRO interface fluency (governance cadences, JOC and JSC governance, escalation handling, change orders); FSP delivery experience (rising signal - 41% of sponsors increased FSP usage 2023-2024 vs 27% FSO); hybrid model navigation (operating across FSP and FSO scopes for the same sponsor); DCT operational experience post FDA Sep 2024 final guidance (CRO investment in home-nursing, telehealth, and digital endpoints has accelerated); named-platform fluency (Medidata Rave, Veeva Vault, Oracle Clinical One, IRT vendors - panels probe specific modules, not generic "EDC experience"); project economics literacy at senior PM and above (study budget management, change order authoring, hour tracking, margin and realization awareness); PMP or equivalent project management certification for senior PM roles; AI/automation literacy (agentic AI for trial monitoring, AI-assisted protocol design, automated risk detection; validation, model oversight, and GCP-aligned AI governance are the framing that translates); TA specialization premium for oncology, rare disease, CGT, and CNS where protocol complexity and patient-access challenges differ materially.

Industry-context awareness: 2025-2026 mid-tier consolidation (Adicon and Crown Bioscience, Worldwide and Catalyst) and top-tier margin pressure (ICON Q3 2025 restructuring and goodwill impairment) shape the hiring market; candidates aware of where consolidation is concentrating capability read as current.

P&L and account growth at PD and above; BD and Strategic Account roles weight win rate, bid-defense conversion, account growth, MSA-to-SOW ratio, and net new awards.

TA framing depends on role: monitoring and operations roles weight TA breadth; specialist roles (medical writing, biostatistics) weight TA depth.

Evidence over claims: CRO panels weight documented sponsor outcomes (deliverables on time, change orders managed without delivery erosion, audits passed, sponsor renewal and expansion, profitability against bid) over adjectival framing.

Hiring-panel de-emphasis falls on: internal-only pharma sponsor experience without external-vendor-management exposure (single-sponsor career history is a flag for portfolio-context roles); tech-build framing without trial-operational outcome; heavy FSO-only background without FSP exposure (noted gap given the sponsor-preference shift); commercial-launch decision-making; sponsor-internal portfolio decisions (CRO does not own product); academic research framing without industry-CRO translation; sales / business-development achievements when the role is operations.

## Adjacency

Translation signal for entries tagged with adjacent industries:

- **pharma**: cross-sponsor portfolio reframes as cross-program oversight; account and SOW management reframes as vendor and contract management; sponsor-oversight-under-R3 vocabulary translates directly. Service-side perspective on the same sponsor-CRO interface pharma owns from the other side. Asset strategy, target product profile, pricing and reimbursement, lifecycle management, and P&L ownership do not carry; these are sponsor-only.
- **biotech**: CRO experience translates strongly - small biotech is the heaviest FSO outsourcer and reads CRO experience as direct operational extension. Frame around "operated as the biotech's clinical operations function" rather than vendor framing. Biotech adds capital and runway constraints and individual scope; the matrix-of-specialists model thins.
- **med-device**: CRO med-device service lines exist (510(k) / PMA / IDE support). ISO 14155 replaces large parts of ICH GCP for device trials; EU MDR (Regulation 2017/745) parallels but differs from the drug-side CTR. Design controls, human factors and usability engineering, ISO 13485 QMS, and ISO 14971 risk management do not carry from drug-CRO work. CRO-side trial-monitoring discipline and statistical rigor carry; device-engineering specificity and pathway-specific submission mechanics (510(k) vs PMA vs De Novo) do not.
- **eclinical**: CRO operations experience is strong adjacency - CRO staff are primary users and implementation partners of eClinical platforms. Frame around vendor selection, validation, integration, and sponsor-SOP-aligned configuration. Veeva Vault, Medidata Rave, Oracle Clinical One, and Florence are current dominant platforms. Going from CRO operations into eClinical product or engineering requires explicit translation of "I used this tool" into "I would build this tool"; SaaS product disciplines (agile and scrum, roadmap ownership, customer success methodology) are not part of CRO work.
- **diagnostics**: CRO central-lab and bioanalytical experience is direct adjacency; CDx studies run in tandem with drug pivotal trials, so CRO oncology biomarker experience (NSCLC PD-L1 combinations and similar) translates into IVD CRO work. ISO 20916 governs clinical performance studies; EU IVDR (Regulation 2017/746) parallels but differs from the drug-side CTR. Analytical and clinical performance characteristics (sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV, LoD, LoQ, precision and reproducibility) are the dominant endpoints rather than the safety and efficacy endpoints of drug CRO work. CLIA and CAP for the clinical lab itself.
- **generics**: sponsor-side oversight of outsourced BE studies (single-dose PK in healthy volunteers, fasted/fed crossover, RSABE for HVD, bioanalytical method validation under GCLP) translates as CRO clin-pharm and bioanalytical service-line experience; GCP, ICH E6(R3), and CDISC SDTM submission discipline carry directly. Specialty BE / clin-pharm CROs are a distinct sub-segment with thin-margin, high-throughput economics. Patient-facing Phase 2/3 site management and TA medical monitoring do not carry; frame as healthy-volunteer Phase 1 / BE operational depth rather than late-phase trial conduct.
