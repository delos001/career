# Diagnostics - CV Framing Rules

**Used by:** cv_targeted, axis-classifier

## Vocabulary

Product categories: in vitro diagnostic (IVD), laboratory developed test (LDT), companion diagnostic (CDx), complementary diagnostic, research use only (RUO), investigational use only (IUO), analyte specific reagent (ASR), point-of-care test (POCT), home-use / over-the-counter (OTC) test, multiplex panel, comprehensive genomic profiling (CGP), liquid biopsy, minimal residual disease (MRD) assay, pharmacogenomics (PGx) test.

Platforms and technologies: immunoassay (ELISA, chemiluminescent immunoassay, electrochemiluminescence), lateral flow immunoassay (LFIA), nucleic acid amplification test (NAAT), qPCR / RT-qPCR, digital PCR (dPCR), isothermal amplification (LAMP, RPA), next-generation sequencing (NGS) including whole-genome and whole-exome (WGS, WES), flow cytometry, mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS, MALDI-TOF), clinical chemistry analyzers, hematology, coagulation, microbiology and blood culture, molecular diagnostics, immunohistochemistry (IHC), in situ hybridization (FISH, CISH), digital pathology.

Specimen and biomarker terms: formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE), plasma, serum, whole blood, urine, bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL), circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA), cell-free DNA (cfDNA), tumor mutational burden (TMB), microsatellite instability (MSI), traceability to reference material, commutability.

Performance and validation terms: analytical verification, analytical validation, clinical validation, clinical performance study, positive percent agreement (PPA), negative percent agreement (NPA), positive predictive value (PPV), negative predictive value (NPV), sensitivity, specificity, limit of blank (LoB), limit of detection (LoD), limit of quantitation (LoQ), analytical measuring interval (AMI), linearity, precision (repeatability, intermediate precision, reproducibility), trueness, bias, cross-reactivity, interference, hook effect, matrix effect, carryover, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) and area under the curve (AUC), coefficient of variation (CV), standard deviation (SD).

Submission and document types: 510(k), De Novo classification, premarket approval (PMA), investigational device exemption (IDE), CLIA Waiver by Application (CW), Pre-Submission (Q-Sub or Pre-Sub), Technical File and Design Dossier (EU IVDR), Performance Evaluation Report (PER), Summary of Safety and Performance (SSP) / Summary of Safety and Clinical Performance (SSCP), Instructions for Use (IFU), labeling per 21 CFR 809.10, Medical Device Report (MDR) under 21 CFR Part 803, Field Safety Corrective Action (FSCA).

Design control and quality work units: design history file (DHF), device master record (DMR), device history record (DHR), risk management file (RMF) per ISO 14971:2019, usability engineering file per IEC 62366, intended use statement, indication for use, post-market surveillance (PMS), post-market performance follow-up (PMPF), vigilance report, Establishment Inspection Report (EIR), 483 observation, warning letter, corrective and preventive action (CAPA), change control, complaint handling, supplier qualification, Unique Device Identifier (UDI).

Regulatory bodies and frameworks: FDA Center for Devices and Radiological Health (CDRH) with the Office of In Vitro Diagnostics (OHT7) within the Office of Product Evaluation and Quality (OPEQ); 21 CFR Part 809 (IVD labeling) and Part 862-866 (classification by analyte/discipline); Quality Management System Regulation (QMSR), effective Feb 2 2026, replacing the Quality System Regulation (QSR, 21 CFR Part 820) and incorporating ISO 13485:2016 by reference with FDA-specific add-ons (labeling, complaint handling, UDI); Clinical Laboratory Improvement Amendments (CLIA) under CMS with CDC scientific support, complexity tiering (waived / moderate / high) per 42 CFR 493.17; College of American Pathologists (CAP) accreditation; EU IVDR (Regulation 2017/746) with class A/B/C/D risk tiering and notified body involvement for B-D and sterile A (transition deadlines Dec 31 2027 for Class D, Dec 31 2028 for Class C, Dec 31 2029 for Classes B and sterile A under 2024 amendment); UK MHRA (UKCA marking), Health Canada, PMDA, NMPA, TGA; Medical Device Single Audit Program (MDSAP) covering Australia, Brazil, Canada, Japan, and the United States (Canada-mandatory since Jan 1 2019).

LDT regulatory posture (live as of 2026-05): FDA's April 2024 final rule to phase out enforcement discretion for LDTs was vacated by a federal court in early 2025 and formally rescinded by FDA on Sept 19 2025; oversight effectively reverts to CLIA / CMS, but the LDT-vs-IVD strategic question remains active in hiring conversations.

Companion diagnostics: regulated as a co-development discipline between CDER or CBER (therapeutic side) and CDRH (device side); separate marketing applications for the drug and the IVD; parallel review pathway available; foundational guidance is the 2014 "In Vitro Companion Diagnostic Devices" final guidance and the 2016 draft "Principles for Codevelopment." Landmark NGS-based CDx include FoundationOne CDx (F1CDx, 324-gene with TMB / MSI), TruSight Oncology Comprehensive, and MI Cancer Seek.

Performance standards: CLSI EP-series anchors analytical evaluation. EP05 (precision of quantitative measurement procedures), EP06 (linearity), EP07 (interference), EP09 (method comparison and bias), EP12 (qualitative test performance; defines PPA and NPA), EP15 (user verification of precision and bias), EP17 (limit of blank, detection, and quantitation), EP25 (stability). ISO 14971:2019 for risk management; ISO 15189:2022 for medical laboratory quality systems; IEC 62304 for medical device software and IEC 62366 for usability engineering.

Software-as-a-medical-device terms: Software as a Medical Device (SaMD), predetermined change control plan (PCCP), locked vs continuously learning models, computer system validation (CSV) or computer software assurance (CSA), 21 CFR Part 11 e-records and e-signatures, ALCOA+ data integrity, interoperability standards (HL7, FHIR, LOINC), laboratory information system (LIS).

Non-preferred terms: tech-industry verbs ("build", "ship", "launch", "iterate") as primary action language for regulated assay work (preferred: develop, verify, validate, transfer, release, commercialize); "customer" as the sole framing for end users (intended user, ordering provider, and payer are distinct stakeholders); "subject" in clinical performance studies (current CLSI and ICH usage favors "participant" or "patient sample donor"); "lab test" alone for regulated IVDs (under-specifies the regulatory class); "AI model" without SaMD classification, lock-state, intended use, and PCCP framing; "innovation" and "disruption" language inside PMA or CE submission contexts.

Preferred-over-alternatives: PPA / NPA over sensitivity / specificity when the comparator is itself imperfect (use sensitivity and specificity only against a recognized gold standard); LoB / LoD / LoQ per CLSI EP17 over "analytical sensitivity"; "intended use" and "indications for use" over "use case"; verification vs validation are not synonyms (verification confirms design inputs are met; validation confirms user needs and clinical claims); "notified body" (EU) vs "FDA review" kept distinct; "companion diagnostic" over "biomarker test" when on-label with a specific therapy; QMSR (current) over QSR for post-Feb-2026 United States framing; ICH E6(R3) where clinical performance studies follow GCP principles.

## Dialect

The industry is acronym-dense across overlapping regulatory, scientific, and platform vocabularies. Acronyms recognized without expansion in diagnostics hiring contexts: IVD, LDT, CDx, FDA, CDRH, OHT7, OPEQ, MHRA, PMDA, NMPA, TGA, IVDR, MDR, MDSAP, CE, UKCA, CLIA, CMS, CAP, CDC, ISO, IEC, CLSI, CFR, PMA, IDE, CW, QSR, QMSR, CAPA, DHF, DMR, DHR, RMF, IFU, SSP, SSCP, PER, PMS, PMPF, FSCA, EIR, UDI, GCP, GxP, ICH, RUO, IUO, ASR, ALCOA, HL7, FHIR, LOINC, LIS, CSV, CSA, SaMD, PCCP, NAAT, PCR, qPCR, dPCR, NGS, WGS, WES, CGP, TMB, MSI, MRD, ctDNA, cfDNA, FFPE, BAL, IHC, FISH, CISH, ELISA, LFIA, LAMP, RPA, POCT, PPA, NPA, PPV, NPV, LoB, LoD, LoQ, AMI, ROC, AUC, OTC, PGx, MALDI, CDER, CBER, AI, ANDA, BD, BLA, CMC, CRO, CTD, CTMS, DEN, EDC, EP, GLP, GMP, IND, IRT, LC, MI, ML, MS, NDA, QMS, REMS, RT, SD, SaaS, TOF, USP, cGMP, eTMF.

## Emphasis

Hiring-panel emphasis falls on: platform-specific depth on a named family (immunoassay, NAAT or qPCR, NGS, mass spec, flow, lateral flow) over generic "assay development" framing; submission and clearance evidence with specific numbers (K-numbers for 510(k), P-numbers for PMA, DEN-numbers for De Novo, CE-IVD certificates, CLIA categorization outcomes, IDE approvals, Pre-Sub meeting outcomes); analytical and clinical performance ownership (authorship of validation plans against named CLSI EP protocols, clinical performance study design, statistical defense of PPA / NPA / LoD / precision claims); design control fluency (DHF ownership, design inputs and outputs traceability, V and V protocols, ISO 14971 risk management, design transfer to manufacturing); QMS scope (ISO 13485 and QMSR working knowledge, surviving FDA inspections, CAP / CLIA inspections, notified body audits, MDSAP audits with named auditing organizations); CDx co-development experience (biomarker selection, assay bridging studies, parallel CDER / CDRH or CBER / CDRH review, contractual co-development structures with named pharma partners where public); IVDR transition execution for EU-facing roles (named notified body relationships, technical file remediation, class up-rating); reagent and manufacturing knowledge (antibody sourcing, conjugate chemistry, primer and probe design, lot-to-lot consistency, raw material qualification, stability program design); clinical and medical credibility for senior roles (board-certified pathologists, CLIA lab directorship eligibility, peer-reviewed publication record).

Regulated work paired with its governing artifact (specific CLSI EP protocol, named submission number, notified body audit outcome, named CDx pharma partner) reads as diagnostics literacy. Generic verbs without assay, submission, platform, or performance numbers attached read as filler.

Evidence over claims: diagnostics panels weight specific assay names with intended use, submission numbers and dates, analytical performance figures with units and study size (LoD in copies per mL, CV under a stated percent, n of validation specimens), the governing CLSI EP document cited, named notified body or accreditor, named pharma partner for CDx work, FDA Pre-Sub outcomes, remediation of named 483 observations or warning letters, IVDR technical file deliverables, design transfer milestones, and lot release throughput over adjectival framing ("led assay development", "responsible for regulatory submissions", "managed quality system", "owned validation").

Hiring-panel de-emphasis falls on: generic "medical device" experience without IVD-specific assay depth (therapeutic-device backgrounds in implants, surgical instruments, or capital equipment do not substitute for assay-side work); pharma or biotech regulatory backgrounds (NDA / BLA experience does not translate cleanly to 510(k) / PMA / IVDR); pure software or SaaS engineering framing for diagnostic SaMD roles without QMSR, IEC 62304, or PCCP language; headcount-as-scope (team size signals less than menu size, throughput, submission cadence, or revenue-bearing assay launches); "innovation" and "disruption" language in regulated-environment contexts.

## Adjacency

Translation signal for entries tagged with adjacent industries:

- **pharma**: GxP discipline (GCP, GLP, cGMP), ICH-aligned development thinking, CDx co-development experience including parallel review and biomarker strategy, clinical trial operations vocabulary, statistical rigor, MDR vigilance reporting as an analog to pharmacovigilance, labeling control, change control, and supplier qualification all carry. NDA / BLA submission mechanics, drug substance and drug product CMC, formulation development, dose-finding pharmacology, and REMS programs do not. Pharma quality systems under 21 CFR 210 and 211 are not interchangeable with QMSR or ISO 13485. Bridging framing maps IVD analogs explicitly: 510(k) corresponds to the marketing-application checkpoint, DHF corresponds to CTD-style traceability, ISO 14971 corresponds to ICH Q9 risk thinking, MDR vigilance corresponds to pharmacovigilance reporting.
- **biotech**: translational science vocabulary (biomarker discovery, target validation, specimen types from FFPE to plasma to whole blood to BAL), molecular biology and assay chemistry foundations, NGS and multi-omics literacy, early-stage scientific judgment, and pharma-facing BD or partnership experience carry. Cell or gene therapy GMP, viral vector manufacturing, bioreactor scale-up, biologics CMC, and IND-enabling tox packages do not. Discovery-stage flexibility does not translate to the design-control discipline an IVD imposes post-feasibility. Bridging framing positions translational and biomarker work as the upstream end of the IVD lifecycle; the conversion point where exploratory assays were moved through RUO to IUO to a regulated IVD or CDx with design controls applied is the signal panels weight.
- **cro**: clinical performance study operations (protocol design, site selection, monitoring, specimen logistics, statistical analysis), GCP fluency, sponsor-facing program-management discipline, regulatory submission support, and central-lab or specialty-lab operations all map directly; many CROs run IVD performance studies and CDx bridging work end-to-end. Sponsor-side product ownership, design controls, manufacturing transfer, post-market surveillance accountability, and assay-menu profit-and-loss responsibility do not carry. Bridging framing cites specific IVD or CDx clinical performance studies run end-to-end with the named sponsor where public, the regulatory deliverable, and the analytical or clinical performance result delivered.
- **med-device**: design controls (DHF, DMR, DHR), ISO 13485 and QMSR fluency, ISO 14971 risk management, 510(k) submission mechanics, IEC 62304 for SaMD, IEC 62366 human factors, post-market surveillance, MDR reporting, complaint handling, CAPA, supplier controls, MDSAP, and UDI all carry. Patient-contacting device design, sterility and biocompatibility (ISO 10993), implantable lifecycle considerations, capital equipment service organizations, and surgical or hospital-floor usability contexts do not. EU MDR (Regulation 2017/745) is parallel to but distinct from IVDR (Regulation 2017/746); regulatory framework names differ even where structure rhymes. Bridging framing leads with the shared CDRH and QMSR spine ("the same design control framework applied to assays rather than instruments or implants") and names IVD-specific deliverables (CLSI EP analytical validation, CLIA categorization, IVDR Performance Evaluation Report) the candidate has owned.
- **eclinical**: software development under regulated conditions (IEC 62304), GxP-validated computer systems (CSV or CSA under 21 CFR Part 11), data integrity per ALCOA+, interoperability standards (HL7, FHIR, LOINC), informatics and bioinformatics pipelines for clinical NGS, LIS work, and SaMD or AI/ML regulatory framing including PCCP guidance carry. Pure eClinical-vendor product mechanics (EDC configuration, eTMF, IRT, CTMS workflows) without diagnostic-context application do not. Sponsor or CRO software cadence is faster and less constrained than the design-control regime an IVD pipeline imposes. Bridging framing positions the work as the informatics or software layer of a regulated diagnostic; name the IVD or CDx the software supported, the QMS it operated under, and the FDA or notified body interaction it survived.
- **generics**: cost-of-goods discipline, manufacturing scale-up and tech transfer, supplier qualification under tight margins, lot-release operations, stability program execution, post-market surveillance, complaint handling, and lean QMS implementation carry. The substantial-equivalence mindset (predicate-based 510(k) reasoning) rhymes loosely with ANDA's reference-listed-drug logic at the strategic level. ANDA and bioequivalence submission mechanics, oral solid dosage manufacturing, dissolution testing, Orange Book familiarity, Hatch-Waxman strategy, and USP or EP monograph compliance do not carry. Generics frameworks (CDER) and IVD frameworks (CDRH and OHT7) share almost no submission-level overlap. Bridging framing leads with operations, quality, and cost work; translate equivalence reasoning carefully, since the strategic rhyme does not extend to mechanics.
