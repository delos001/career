# Med-Device - CV Framing Rules

**Used by:** cv_targeted, axis_classifier

## Vocabulary

Submission and clearance terms: 510(k) (premarket notification, substantial-equivalence to predicate), PMA (premarket approval, Class III), De Novo (novel low/moderate-risk, no predicate), IDE (investigational device exemption), HDE (humanitarian device exemption) for HUDs (humanitarian use devices), eSTAR (mandatory submission format for 510(k) and De Novo), Refuse-to-Accept (RTA - obsoleted in eSTAR workflow). Q-Submission program (per FDA May 2025 final guidance): Pre-Submission (Pre-Sub), Submission Issue Request (SIR), Study Risk Determination, Informational Meeting.

Classification and lifecycle terms: Class I, Class II, Class III; risk-based classification; SR / NSR (significant risk / non-significant risk) study determinations; predicate device; substantial equivalence; total product life cycle (TPLC); intended use; indications for use.

Design-control terms (21 CFR 820.30, retained verbatim under QMSR): design and development plan, design inputs, design outputs, design review, design verification, design validation, design transfer, design changes, Design History File (DHF), traceability matrix, user needs.

Quality system - QSR/QMSR transition: QMSR (Quality Management System Regulation) - final rule Jan 2024, technical amendments Dec 2025, effective Feb 2, 2026; incorporates ISO 13485:2016 by reference plus clause 3 of ISO 9000:2015. QSIT (Quality System Inspection Technique) withdrawn Feb 2, 2026; replaced by Compliance Program 7382.850. Vocabulary state in 2026: QMSR is the current term; QSR appears only in legacy/historical context; "QSR-aligned" or "QSR-compliant" framing reads as outdated. ISO 13485:2016 active; revision in process. Risk management (ISO 14971) explicitly integrated throughout - explicit risk-management language expected.

Post-market terms: Medical Device Reporting (MDR - note acronym collision with EU MDR; context-dependent), 21 CFR Part 803 reporting timelines (30-day, 5-day); Reports of Corrections and Removals, 21 CFR Part 806; complaint handling (21 CFR 820.198 / ISO 13485 §8.2.2); recall; field action; post-market surveillance (PMS); post-market clinical follow-up (PMCF - EU); periodic safety update report (PSUR - EU MDR); UDI/GUDID traceability for scoping affected product; nonconformance; CAPA; Form 483; Warning Letter.

Clinical evaluation terms: clinical evaluation; Clinical Evaluation Report (CER - EU-required, central to MDR); clinical investigation; pivotal study; ISO 14155 (GCP for device studies); investigator's brochure; clinical study report (CSR); Unanticipated Adverse Device Effect (UADE).

Stakeholders: FDA CDRH (Center for Devices and Radiological Health); CBER (overlaps for combination/biologic devices); Notified Body (NB); EU Competent Authority; EUDAMED; sponsor; contract manufacturer; OEM.

Regulatory frameworks: 21 CFR Part 820 (QMSR); ISO 13485:2016; ISO 14971 (risk management); IEC 62304 (medical device software lifecycle, with safety classes A/B/C); ISO 14155 (clinical investigations GCP); IEC 62366 (usability engineering); ISO 10993 (biocompatibility); EU MDR 2017/745; EU IVDR 2017/746; 21 CFR Part 11 (electronic records, shared with pharma).

Regulatory bodies: FDA CDRH, EU Commission / Notified Bodies, MHRA (UK), PMDA (Japan), NMPA (China), Health Canada, TGA (Australia), ANVISA (Brazil); IMDRF (International Medical Device Regulators Forum - harmonization, not a regulator).

Operating concepts: design controls; risk management (per ISO 14971); software lifecycle (per IEC 62304); cybersecurity (per FDA Sep 2023 final guidance "Cybersecurity in Medical Devices: Quality System Considerations", updated Jun 2025); SBOM (Software Bill of Materials, statutorily required under FD&C §524B); UDI / DI / PI (Unique Device Identification, Device Identifier, Production Identifier); predicate device; substantial equivalence; total product life cycle (TPLC); real-world data and evidence (RWD/RWE - see emphasis); predetermined change control plan (PCCP).

Designations: Breakthrough Devices Program (1,246 designations granted, 185 marketing authorizations as of Dec 31, 2025); Safer Technologies Program for Medical Devices (STeP - for less-serious conditions with significant safety improvement); both reduce time-to-market and provide enhanced FDA interaction.

SaMD / AI-ML terms: Software as a Medical Device (SaMD); AI-enabled device software function (AI-DSF - current FDA preferred term, broader than ML-only); Predetermined Change Control Plan (PCCP - final guidance Dec 2024, scope expanded from ML-only to all AI); TPLC for AI-DSFs (Jan 2025 draft); Clinical Decision Support (CDS - Jan 2026 update with deregulatory posture for low-risk); IMDRF SaMD WG/N81 (Jan 2025 characterization framework). Note: FDA withdrew adoption of IMDRF SaMD Clinical Evaluation principles early 2026 - citing IMDRF SaMD Clinical Evaluation as governing FDA framework is now incorrect.

Non-preferred terms: "drug" or "compound" framing for therapeutic products; "GxP" generically (med-device uses ISO/CFR-specific frameworks, not GMP/GCP shorthand outside clinical-trial work); "patient" where "user" or "operator" is the actual stakeholder; "ship" / "iterate" without design-controls grounding; "QSR" or "QSR-compliant" as live framework (reads dated post Feb 2026); citing IMDRF SaMD Clinical Evaluation as governing FDA framework.

## Dialect

Acronyms recognized in med-device hiring contexts without expansion: FDA, CDRH, CBER, EMA, MHRA, PMDA, NMPA, TGA, IMDRF, 510(k), PMA, De Novo, IDE, HDE, HUD, QSR, QMSR, QSIT, CGMP, ISO 13485, ISO 14971, ISO 14155, IEC 62304, IEC 62366, ISO 10993, MDR (context-dependent), IVDR, EU MDR, CER, PMCF, PMS, PSUR, DHF, DMR, DHR, CAPA, NCR, OOT, OOS, SR, NSR, RTA, eSTAR, Q-Sub, Pre-Sub, SIR, UDI, DI, PI, GUDID, UDID, SaMD, AI-DSF, PCCP, TPLC, SBOM, NB, CE, MDD, AIMDD, IVDD, GMDN, GS1, AdvaMed, MedTech Europe, BSI, TÜV, RWD, RWE, STeP, BDP.

## Emphasis

Hiring-panel emphasis falls on, in descending order:

Design-controls fluency is the foundational signal. 21 CFR 820.30 traceability - user needs → design inputs → outputs → V&V → transfer - and DHF authorship/maintenance is expected of any R&D, RA, or QA candidate. Common 483/Warning Letter weaknesses (poor traceability, weak independent review, document control gaps) are routine interview probes.

Quality-system rigor under QMSR (post-Feb-2026): candidates should frame work in QMSR / ISO 13485:2016 terms, with explicit risk-management integration (ISO 14971) - not generic "quality systems" language. QSIT-era inspection vocabulary signals dated experience; new Compliance Program 7382.850 inspection familiarity is current.

Post-market literacy: MDR reporting timelines (30-day, 5-day), Part 806 corrections and removals, complaint handling, CAPA, recall scoping using UDI traceability. EU MDR PMS/PMCF/PSUR cycle is a separate, additive signal.

SaMD / AI-ML literacy where role-relevant: PCCP authorship; IEC 62304 software lifecycle with safety class designation (A/B/C); cybersecurity per the Sep 2023 final guidance - SBOM, threat modeling, vulnerability management - now a cross-functional expectation, not just security-team work, since §524B is statutory and applies to any cyber device submission. Reciting IMDRF SaMD Clinical Evaluation as governing reads as a negative signal post-2026 withdrawal.

Submission-pathway exposure: 510(k) volume / clearance experience; De Novo (signals novel-device work); PMA (signals high-risk / Class III depth); IDE study experience (signals device-clinical bridge). Q-Sub / Pre-Sub interaction is current vocabulary; eSTAR fluency is now baseline.

EU dual-track readiness: EU MDR / IVDR experience reads as additive scope. Class III & IIb implantable transition deadline is Dec 31, 2027; Class IIb/IIa/Class I sterile/measuring is Dec 31, 2028 (per Regulation (EU) 2023/607, conditional on QMS compliance and notified-body application milestones - extended legacy-device transition dates, not absolute compliance walls); IVDR Class C is May 26, 2026; Class B / A-sterile is Dec 31, 2029. CER authorship and PMCF planning are typical EU-side anchors.

RWD/RWE: per FDA Dec 2025 final guidance (effective Feb 17, 2026 for submission expectations), RWE is now formally accepted across 510(k), De Novo, PMA, PMA supplements, HDE, IDE, and post-approval studies; aggregate / de-identified data is acceptable without individual-record access. RWE experience is increasingly a differentiator, not a niche skill.

Designations: Breakthrough Devices and STeP designations on a CV signal high-touch FDA interaction and are non-trivial credentials.

Evidence over claims: med-device panels weight documented submissions cleared, designations earned, audits passed, recalls managed, CAPAs closed, post-market signals tracked over adjectival framing.

Hiring-panel de-emphasis falls on: pharma trial-phase vocabulary (Phase I/II/III) without device translation; CDISC/SDTM/ADaM (irrelevant unless device involves combination product or registry analytics); "ICH GCP" without clarifying ISO 14155 device-specific GCP; pure software-product-management framing without design-controls / IEC 62304 anchoring.

## Adjacency

Translation signal for entries tagged with adjacent industries:

- **pharma**: design-controls discipline maps loosely to GMP rigor; ISO 13485 maps to GMP; ISO 14971 risk management is a strong differentiator (predates ICH Q9 thinking); 510(k)/PMA submission discipline reads as regulatory submission rigor - pharma expects framework attribution (CTD/eCTD), so name the device equivalent. ISO 14155 maps to ICH E6 GCP - call out the equivalence explicitly. Combination-product or drug-device experience is the natural bridge.
- **biotech**: same logic as pharma; combination products (drug-eluting devices, prefilled delivery systems) are the strongest bridge. Smaller-scope, multi-hat work translates directly.
- **cro**: sponsor-side device experience translates as program oversight; ISO 14155 GCP maps to ICH E6. Device-CRO sub-sector exists but is narrower; positioning as "regulated-trial oversight" carries.
- **diagnostics**: IVDR / IVDD / LDT and 510(k) IVD experience is a near-direct translation; ISO 13485 carries; design-controls vocabulary carries. Assay-development specifics do not translate to therapeutic-device contexts.
- **eclinical**: IEC 62304 software-lifecycle and cybersecurity (FDA Sep 2023) experience translates as regulated-software discipline; 21 CFR Part 11 is shared. SaMD specifically maps as the device-side analog of regulated clinical software platforms. PCCP discipline is a differentiator - eclinical has nothing equivalent yet.
