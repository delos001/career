# CV rebuild: whole-picture inventory

Companion to `cv_persuasion_plan.md`. Written 2026-11-12 so the next session starts from one place. Why the rebuild exists (the user's words): the CVs were not persuasive; the writing was guided by excluding what was not correct instead of including the persuasive experience that gets an interview. Persuasion stays the top test (`cv_edit_principles.md`).

## How each kind is treated (user, 2026-11-12)

- **Conflicts:** the old rules were not optimized for persuasion, so a legacy rule is a target to improve, especially where it conflicts with a principle or the plan.
- **Constraints:** keep them, but group them into retrievable sections the writer reads just in time, or split the architect into pre-architect steps, or another method. Decided under Q1 below.
- **Retire:** anything obsolete, that fails the objectives or hinders them, is cleaned out, so it stops costing context.
- Walk all of it so nothing is lost.

## Q1, decide first: the architect's workload

The user's concern: the architect reads a lot (JD, jd_needs, five axis files, research, writing rules, gap analysis, retrieval, inventory, more), much of it long prose, then must write prose that is both accurate and persuasive, not copied inventory entries, as a good start for the reviewers. That may be too much for one agent's context and attention.

Why first: the answer decides the shape of step 3 (how `cv-structure.md` is split) and step 4 (one architect or several steps).

**Measured (Jazz APP-028 architect transcript, 2026-11-06 run):** about 214,000 tokens of input before drafting; inventory, narratives and retrieval are about 70% of it, the writing rules about 15%. The CV cited 79 of 256 EX entries; retrieval listed all 401 candidate rows with one overall score each and no need recorded. The continued architect reached 429,559 tokens; the money cost was mostly cached re-reads, so the issue is attention, not cost.

**Decided (user, 2026-11-12):** a plan with five parts, all adopted together.
1. Read the profile once. The step that reads the profile (retrieval today) records which needs each entry fits; downstream steps read that per-need subset of entries, or pull a section (roles and dates, education, certifications) by script. Nothing downstream reads the whole inventory or narratives. The subset is generous: the long draft (plan decision 10) needs more than it keeps, and scores carried forward break ties in the cut.
2. Separate drafting steps: a plan step holds the full picture (claims per need, role per claim); one writer drafts all of Professional Experience; a second writes the summary and Core Competencies after it, from the finished experience section; a script fills Earlier Professional Roles and credentials; an assembly step joins them. Repetition across sections is a known risk the assembly and reviewers check.
3. Each revision is done by a fresh agent reading the draft, the plan, the findings and only the rules and subset its sections need. The plan carries one "why" line where a choice is not obvious from the need tag and citation.
4. Retire candidates are cut from the files writers and reviewers read (`cv-structure.md`, agent files, `cv-best-practices.md`) and marked superseded in `design_decisions.md`, in plan step 3.
5. `cv-structure.md` is split into sections a script pulls one at a time, so each step reads only its own rules, in plan step 3.
