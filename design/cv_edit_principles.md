# CV edit principles (plan step 2)

Confirmed by the user one at a time from his hand edits, per `cv_persuasion_plan.md` step 2. Each is a judgment principle: its intent and when it applies. Persuasion is the test for every one: does the text make a reviewer want to interview him, accurately.

No principle may contradict the axis files (`rules/<axis>/`); where one interacts with an axis, it defers to that axis file. If an axis file's instruction proves weak, the fix is a change to that axis file, not a rule that overrides it.

Source key: the application whose edit showed it; a later application adds itself when it shows the same edit.

## Confirmed

1. **Summary built from the role statement** (Jazz, Summit). Each summary sentence shows he has done one key item of the role statement, rolled up across roles, written to persuade; specific results belong in the bullets. Intent: in the first seconds a reader matches him to the job's core functions and wants to read on. Plan decision 2.
2. **Every bullet shows its match to a need** (Jazz, Summit, WCT, Genmab). Each bullet names the function it shows the way the JD names that kind of work. The opening is judged per bullet: the function when the match is the selling point ("Led development of a reusable dashboard template..."), the impact when the result is the stronger draw and still shows the function. Detail that serves no need comes out. Intent: the reader sees "has done what we need" in every bullet, by whichever opening persuades more. Plan decision 8.
3. **Each role is built from the job's needs** (Jazz, Summit, Faro). For each role, start from the needs and ask which ones this role can show; write a bullet for each, even if no single inventory entry reads that way. Where related entries together show broader scope or responsibility, merge them into one claim ("developed standardization libraries covering templates, vendors and programming, used across the study and vendor portfolio"); the merged claim cites every entry it draws on and claims nothing none of them shows. Whether to merge follows the level file: leadership seniority reads as breadth and reach (`rules/levels/leadership.md`), so merging usually helps; IC seniority reads as depth (`rules/levels/ic.md`), and a need asking for varied work keeps varied bullets. Cut bullets that serve no need, even strong ones. Intent: the CV covers the job's needs at the largest scale the evidence and the level support. Plan decisions 8 and 11a.
4. **Bullet order: related together, by need rank** (Jazz, Vertex, Faro). Within a role, related bullets sit together so they read as one area of breadth; groups and lone bullets run in the order of the needs they serve, highest-ranked first, a group taking the place of its highest-ranked bullet. Grouping adjacent bullets and merging into one (principle 3) are two ways to show the same breadth; the level file and space decide which. Intent: the first bullets go to what the job values most, and related work reads as connected, never scattered. Plan decision 4.
5. **The JD's term when it is accurate** (Jazz). When the JD has its own term for work he did, use the JD's term, if and only if it accurately describes what he did; otherwise keep the source's term. Example: the entry said "data conformance", the matching Jazz duty said "programming and reporting", which was accurate, so the bullet used "programming and reporting". Intent: the reader sees their own words for work he has actually done. Plan decision 7.
6. **Natural phrasing** (Jazz). Write the way a professional would say it: standard word pairings ("advise against", not "advise out of"), and a benefit stated plainly ("make decisions from current data"), never a staged scene ("decided in the meeting", "a team member took it over"), and plain verbs for what he did ("interviewed and provided recommendations on 50+ candidates, resulting in ... hires", not "evaluated 50+ candidates behind ... hires"). Such details serve no job. Intent: nothing reads as odd or invented; an odd phrase stops the reader and a staged one invites doubt. Extends the first-pass clarity rule in `cv-structure.md`.
7. **Older roles are evidence too** (Jazz, Summit, WCT). When an older role shows a job need, especially ownership, accountability or adoption, its bullet says so with the scope that proves it, even when the role gets one or two lines ("held end-to-end accountability ... including clinical trial management team, and in-sourcing and outsourcing delivery"; "drove Infosario Analytics adoption ... improving platform utilization"). Intent: the career reads as long-running depth in what the job needs, not recent work plus a list of past titles.
8. **No lower step beside a higher one** (Jazz, Summit, WCT, Vertex). When a higher step in the same line is listed, the lower step is left off by default: an associate's degree beside a bachelor's, a Green Belt beside a Black Belt. It adds a line and little signal.
10. **Current work in Core Competencies** (Summit, WCT, Vertex, Genmab). When the job asks for a capability his current independent work shows (e.g. AI building), Core Competencies names it in the JD's terms even if past roles do not ("AI driven USDM and regulation knowledge graphing"). Intent: the reader sees the capability is current and hands-on, not only evaluated. Extends plan decision 3 to Core Competencies.
11. **"Led", not "operationally led"** (WCT, Jazz). Leadership is stated as "led", with no qualifier such as "operationally", "functionally" or "matrix-"; where it matters, the team or setting carries the detail ("led a global matrix team of seven data managers"). Wording limits still hold: never "line management" or bare "direct reports". Intent: a qualified verb reads as "led, but not really" and undersells leading people.
12. **Independent Projects** (WCT). Current self-directed work sits under "Independent Projects", most recent first, each with its true date range. Intent: the reader sees continuous, current, hands-on work, framed honestly as his own.
13. **No contrasts that could read as critical** (Vertex). Leave out contrasts that could read as critical or combative of other approaches, past employers or how things are usually done ("through matrix influence rather than headcount"); those points belong in the interview, where tone can be softened and context given. A plain outcome stays ("absorbed 200+ transfers without headcount growth" reports a result and judges no one). Intent: the CV reads as objective and confident, never as an argument.
14. **Bullets light enough to read in one pass** (Faro, Summit, Genmab; his wife's read: in the industry, outside his area, the CVs read well only when bullets were concise and not dense). A bullet carries one main claim and at most one or two supporting details; every clause earns its place against the reader's effort, and true detail the claim does not need is cut. A starting point ("from roughly 60% to zero") stays when it is the impact and the bullet still fits two lines; otherwise give the end result. Test: a smart reader outside his specialty gets the point in one pass. Intent: a skimming reader keeps reading because every bullet is easy. Open for step 3: whether `cv-structure.md`'s 3-line allowance (line 161) survives.
9. **Where space comes from** (Jazz, Summit, WCT, Vertex, Faro). When space forces a cut, first compress in older roles, then in newer roles (merge per principle 3, tighten wording). If something must still go, cut the bullet serving the lowest-ranked need; between bullets of similar weight, cut from the older role, since much of page 2 goes unread, but keep the line showing that role's need (principle 7). Recent roles keep their depth. In-progress training is an early cut when space is short. The writer never stacks titles at one employer (`cv-structure.md` line 133 stands: unstacking by hand is hard). Stacking ("Global and Regional Clinical Trial Manager", combined dates) is the user's hand edit when space is short, the role is not central to the job, and each bullet plainly belongs to one title (Faro); when the role matters he keeps titles separate even at a cost elsewhere (Amgen on others). Intent: protect what the reader actually sees. Plan decision 10.
